# LessonLock — Facebook Group Survey (No-Show Research)

**Goal:** Validate demand for no-show protection among online music teachers. Target: 100+ responses. Kill-gate pre-check: if <50% say they'd require card-on-file → revisit positioning.

**How to use:** Paste the survey intro + questions into a Google Form / Typeform / Tally, then post the link in the Facebook groups below. Keep the form under ~3 minutes (12 questions). Offer to share aggregated results as the incentive.

---

## Facebook post copy (paste into group)

> Hi everyone — I'm researching how online music teachers handle no-shows and late cancels. I'm a founder building tools for teachers, not a salesperson. Would you spare 3 minutes for a quick anonymous survey? No pitch, no spam. I'll share the aggregated results back to this group so we can all see what's working.

## Survey questions (Google Form / Typeform)

### Intro screen
"Thanks for taking 3 minutes. This is anonymous research on how online music teachers handle no-shows and late cancels. No product pitch — results shared back to the group."

### Section 1 — You
1. **What do you teach?** (single choice) Piano / Guitar / Voice / Drums / Strings / Other
2. **How many students do you teach?** (single choice) 1–5 / 6–15 / 16–30 / 30+
3. **Average lesson rate (per hour, USD)?** (short text or number)
4. **What % of lessons get cancelled late or no-show in a typical month?** (single choice) 0% / 1–5% / 6–15% / 16–30% / 30%+

### Section 2 — Current situation
5. **How do you currently handle late cancels / no-shows?** (multiple choice) I have a written policy / I charge a fee / I chase payment manually / I do nothing / Other
6. **How much time per week do you spend chasing payments or rebooking?** (single choice) None / <1 hr / 1–3 hrs / 3+ hrs
7. **Have you ever lost money to a no-show?** (single choice) Yes / No
8. **Roughly how much have you lost to no-shows in the last 12 months?** (single choice) $0 / $1–$100 / $101–$500 / $501–$1,000 / $1,000+

### Section 3 — Tools & pain
9. **What tools do you use for booking/scheduling/payments?** (multiple choice) Calendly / Acuity / Stripe / PayPal / Venmo / Zelle / Cash / Other
10. **What's the most frustrating part of your current setup?** (short text, optional)
11. **Have you ever tried requiring a card on file or a deposit?** (single choice) Yes, it worked / Yes, it backfired / No, never tried / No, but I've considered it

### Section 4 — Card-on-file reaction
12. **"What if students put a card on file once when booking, and you're automatically paid a kept fee (e.g. 50% of the lesson) if they late-cancel or no-show — with a 1-click refund if you want to forgive it?"** (scale 1–5: 1 = "No way" … 5 = "I'd use this today")
13. **What concerns would you have?** (multiple choice) Parent pushback / Trust issues / Processing fees / Too complex / None / Other
14. **Would you require card-on-file for all new students, or only after a no-show?** (single choice) All new students / Only after a no-show / Never
15. **What's a fair kept-fee % and cancel window for your studio?** (short text, e.g. "50% if <24 hrs")

### Section 5 — Pricing & follow-up
16. **If this existed today, what would you pay monthly?** (single choice) $0 / $12 / $29 / $49 / Other
17. **Would you want SMS reminders to students, or is email enough?** (single choice) SMS + email / Email only / Neither
18. **Can I follow up in a few weeks? (optional email)** (short text)

### Optional open field
19. **Anything else you'd like to share?** (long text, optional)

## Survey vs. call — recommendation

**Do both, but lead with calls for the first 10.**

- **Calls (15 min, 1-on-1)** give you *why*: exact quotes, objections, tone, and the magic-wand answers that shape positioning. You need these to write copy that converts. Calls also build relationships you can re-engage at launch.
- **Facebook survey** gives you *how many*: breadth, distribution of no-show %, WTP, and a cheap way to hit the kill-gate with volume. But surveys lose nuance — people misreport, and you can't ask follow-ups.
- **Practical sequence:** Run 10 calls first (validates the questions + gives quotes), then post the survey to 3–4 groups to scale to 100+ and confirm the kill-gate. Use the survey's open-text answers to spot patterns, then do 5 more calls with people who answered interestingly.

**Tips for the FB post:** post in the morning (Tue–Thu), include "no pitch" prominently, offer to share results, and reply to every comment within an hour. Group admins often require approval — DM the admin first. Don't post the same link twice in one group.
